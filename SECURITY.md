# Security policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

Do not open a public issue for a security problem. Report it privately through the contact form on the company website:

<https://fjeldstadconsulting.ai/#kontakt>

The form is the same one clients use, so it asks what you are trying to achieve and what the problem is. Write in the problem field, start with the words "Security report", and describe the problem in general terms. The form needs your name, an e-mail address to reply to and a tick in its consent box, which covers using what you write to answer you. We reply by e-mail and agree with you how to send the details.

In the form, give a short summary and say which repository or page it concerns. In the reply, when we have agreed how to send it, tell us:

- what you found;
- where it is: the repository, file and commit, or the address of the page;
- how to reproduce it;
- what you think the impact is.

The form sends what you write as ordinary e-mail, which is not encrypted end to end, and takes about 5,000 characters. Leave out passwords, keys, exploit code, personal data about other people and any data you obtained. If the report needs such material, say so, and we agree on how to send it.

## What happens next

1. You get a reply that the report has arrived, normally within five working days.
2. We check the report and tell you whether we can reproduce it. There is no fixed time for this.
3. A confirmed problem is fixed, or we tell you how it is handled. A fix is reviewed before it is published.
4. We tell you when the fix is out. If you want to be named as the finder, say so.

Fjeldstad Consulting is a small company and has no bounty programme.

## Scope

All repositories in the Fjeldstad-Consulting organisation and the services they deploy. A repository that has been handed over to a client is out of scope; report to the client.

## What not to do

This policy does not give permission to test, and Fjeldstad Consulting offers no legal safe harbour. Do not test a service that runs for a client; report what you observed. Never test against production systems with real customer data, attempt denial of service or access data that is not yours. When you have come across a problem, stop there and report it.
